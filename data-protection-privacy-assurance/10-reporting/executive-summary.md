# Executive Summary: Data Protection and Privacy Assurance

> **Portfolio project.** This is an independent learning project using a fictional organisation and illustrative data. It is not an audit, legal opinion, or certification.

All results, dates and decisions are scenario results for the fictional Club.

**The problem.** Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional Kenyan members' club, processed member, staff and visitor data across cloud systems, CCTV, number-plate recognition and an AI chatbot without a record of processing, DPIAs, processor agreements, a retention schedule or a breach plan.

**What was done.** 14 processing activities were recorded with their purpose, lawful basis, systems, recipients and retention, and their data flows mapped. DPIAs were completed for gate CCTV with ANPR and for the Kijani Assist chatbot, and the Committee approved both with conditions. Processor agreements and a cross-border transfer assessment, a retention schedule, a privacy notice and CCTV signs, a request procedure and a breach response plan followed.

**What was found.** Five of six October findings are closed. CCTV retention fell from 180 to 30 days and 412 old member files were shredded. All four requests in the quarter were answered on time, and the first breach was assessed and closed within a week. Five smaller findings remain, mainly two missing processor agreements and staff training.

**What is recommended.** Sign the remaining two agreements, finish disposal of old records by January 2027, and train all staff who handle personal data by March 2027.

**What this demonstrates.** Applying the Kenyan Data Protection Act to a real-world operation: lawful bases, DPIAs with measured residual risk, processor and transfer safeguards, retention in practice, and a working breach process.
