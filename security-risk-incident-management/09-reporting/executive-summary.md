# Executive Summary: Security Risk and Incident Management

> **Portfolio project.** This is an independent learning project using a fictional organisation and illustrative data. It is not an audit, legal opinion, or certification.

All results, dates, accounts and events are scenario results for the fictional Club. No real systems, configurations or vulnerabilities are described.

**The problem.** Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional Kenyan members' club, rated a personal data breach through cloud systems or supplier access as its highest risk, but had never reviewed who could access its systems, tested its response to an incident, or tested recovery.

**What was done.** 14 assets were rated and 11 threats assessed into a security risk register, and the site was surveyed. 24 privileged and member-data accounts were reviewed, and 40 office staff received an authorised phishing simulation. An incident response plan, severity matrix, three playbooks and a chain-of-custody form were written and tested in a tabletop exercise based on a phishing attack and member data theft. A business impact analysis set recovery objectives for seven processes, and four recovery tests followed.

**What was found.** Only 58% of accounts were appropriate: 4 leavers were still active and 2 supplier admin accounts had no MFA (both fixed within a week). 23% of staff clicked the phishing link. In the exercise the Club met the 72-hour ODPC deadline (about 50 hours) but warned members after about 52 hours. Two of four recovery tests met their targets.

**What is recommended.** Named accounts for reception, supplier alerts and out-of-hours contacts, a pre-approved member warning, staff awareness training, a spare CCTV recorder and better coverage of the fuel store.

**What this demonstrates.** Risk-based security assessment across physical and information security, access control in practice, incident readiness tested against legal deadlines, and continuity planning measured by real tests.
