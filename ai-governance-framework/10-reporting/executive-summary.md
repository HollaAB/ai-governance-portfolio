# Executive Summary: AI Governance Framework

> **Portfolio project.** This is an independent learning project using a fictional organisation and illustrative data. It is not an audit, legal opinion, or certification.

All results, dates and decisions are scenario results for the fictional Club.

**The problem.** Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional Kenyan members' club, launched an AI chatbot and number-plate recognition with no AI governance. Nobody had listed its AI systems, judged their risk or tested them.

**What was done.** An AI governance charter set roles and a lifecycle from proposal to retirement. Seven AI systems were inventoried and classified on six criteria. The chatbot received an impact assessment covering six harms and was tested with 150 prompts before and after fixes. A human oversight matrix, a staff acceptable-use policy, vendor due diligence, and model change and incident registers followed.

**What was found.** Version 1.0 of the chatbot shared balances after weak checks in 3 of 30 tests and leaked other members' data in 2 of 20 adversarial tests. Version 1.1 passed all account and adversarial tests and raised overall accuracy to 96%. Swahili accuracy (85%) is still below target. A vendor CV screening tool was rejected.

**What is recommended.** Approve the charter, chatbot version 1.1 and opt-in ANPR; fix Swahili accuracy and obtain the vendor's security certificate by early 2027.

**What this demonstrates.** Practical AI governance: inventory and risk-tiering, impact assessment with measurable residual risk, evidence-based testing before approval, human oversight, and third-party AI controls.
