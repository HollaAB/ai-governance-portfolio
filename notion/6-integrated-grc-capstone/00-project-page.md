## 1. Executive Summary

The five earlier projects each covered one function at Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional Kenyan members' club. A single phishing incident, the "Saturday Medal" exercise, crosses all of them at once. I joined the projects around that incident: 16 key requirements were traced from law or policy to control, test, finding and action; risks from every project were combined against the Club's appetite; shared root causes became five cross-cutting findings; and every open action went into one remediation roadmap with a dashboard and a Committee paper. Compliance rose from 18 to 25 of 36 obligations and every High compliance finding is closed, but member warnings, supplier support, account security, staff awareness and recovery still fall short, and 6 of 18 enterprise risks remain outside appetite (scenario results).

## 2. The Problem

**Problem statement:** Compliance, governance, data protection, AI and security work had been done project by project, but a real incident does not respect those boundaries.

**Why it matters:** In the exercise, a phished shared password led to the export of 1,850 members' contacts and fake payment requests. Each function had part of the answer, and the gaps sat at the joins between them. A board needs one view and one plan, not five.

## 3. Objectives

The project aims to:

- Trace key requirements end to end, from source to control, test, finding and action.
- Combine risks from every project into one register against the Club's appetite.
- Find root causes shared across functions.
- Consolidate every open action into one roadmap the Management Committee can approve and track.

## 4. Scope and Assumptions

**Included:** An integrated case study and evidence trail; a requirements-to-controls mapping and integrated risk and control register; cross-cutting findings and corrective actions; one remediation roadmap; a portfolio dashboard and Committee paper; an executive report, presentation outline and lessons learned.

**Excluded:** New testing. The capstone uses the results already recorded in the five projects.

## 5. Methodology

**My approach:**

1. Used the "Saturday Medal" incident as the thread, because it touches every function.
2. For each requirement the incident tested, followed the chain: requirement, control, test evidence, result, finding, corrective action, status.
3. Combined the enterprise risk register with the controls and open findings from all projects.
4. Grouped project findings that share a cause into cross-cutting findings.
5. Collected every open action from the five projects into one roadmap, phased by due date, and reported it on one dashboard, in a Committee paper and in an executive report.

**Standards, frameworks, or references used:** The frameworks used in the earlier projects, linked together: ISO 37301 (compliance), ISO 37000 (governance), ISO 31000 (risk), ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF (AI), and NIST CSF 2.0 (security); the Data Protection Act 2019.

## 7. Testing Results or Evaluation Criteria

The capstone evaluates the Club's position across all projects. All results are scenario results.

- **Requirements traced:** 16; 9 met, 2 partly met, 5 not met.
- **Obligations compliant:** 25 of 36 (69%), up from 18 of 36 in October 2026; none not compliant.
- **Findings:** 16 of 44 project findings closed; no High finding open at project level.
- **Risk:** 6 of 18 enterprise risks outside appetite, down from 9.
- **Incident readiness:** the Data Protection Commissioner was notified at about 50 hours (target 72, met); members were warned at about 52 hours (target 24, not met).

## 8. Key Findings or Design Decisions

**Finding 1: The same weakness appears in several places.** Shared accounts, supplier support and staff awareness each showed up in two or three projects. Grouping them into five cross-cutting findings means each is fixed once.

**Finding 2: Incidents expose the joins.** The exercise met the legal deadline but failed on a decision nobody owned: the delegation of authority did not cover warning members, so fraud continued for two days.

**Decision 3: One roadmap for the whole Club.** All 28 open actions from five projects sit in one roadmap, in two phases, reported on one dashboard. The Committee approves and tracks one plan instead of five.

## 9. Practical Recommendations

| Recommendation | Rationale | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Approve the integrated roadmap of 28 open actions | One plan with owners and dates replaces five separate trackers | High |
| Delegate pre-approved member fraud warnings to the DPO | Members were warned after about 52 hours, against a 24-hour target | High |
| Finish named accounts with MFA for every user of member data | A shared password without MFA let the attacker in | High |
| Add alerting, out-of-hours contact and incident support terms to critical supplier contracts | Suppliers did not alert the Club or answer at the weekend | Medium |
| Fund one combined awareness programme and the continuity fixes | Three projects found the same training gap; recovery targets are unmet | Medium |

## 10. Implementation Roadmap

- **Phase 1, by March 2027 (21 actions):** named accounts with MFA and delegated member warnings (February); supplier incident terms (March); the remaining compliance, data protection, AI and governance actions due in this period.
- **Phase 2, April to June 2027 (7 actions):** combined training programme, backup decision and spare recorder (April), and the remaining security and compliance actions.
- **Every quarter (Compliance Officer):** report progress to the Finance and Audit sub-committee; the Management Committee reviews the portfolio dashboard.

## 11. Skills Demonstrated

- **Integrated GRC:** connecting compliance, governance, data protection, AI and security in one business scenario.
- **Traceability:** following each requirement from source to control, test, finding and action.
- **Root-cause analysis:** grouping findings across functions into shared causes.
- **Executive communication:** one dashboard, a Committee paper and an executive report a board can act on.

## 12. Lessons Learned

New technology carried most of the risk, and many compliance gaps had a governance cause. Testing changed decisions: the chatbot looked fine until structured tests showed it leaked data. Keeping one obligations register and one risk register across projects made the evidence traceable end to end. In a real organisation, the roadmap would need budget approval and the figures would change as actions close.

## 13. Next Steps

- Take the Committee paper to the Management Committee (February 2027, scenario) for the five decisions.
- Track the roadmap quarterly on the portfolio dashboard.
- Repeat the incident exercise once the Phase 1 actions are closed.
