## 1. Purpose

To give the Committee one view of the Club's position after five months of work, report the lessons of the January incident exercise, and ask for approval of one remediation roadmap.

## 3. What the exercise showed

The Club would meet the ODPC's 72-hour deadline, but members would be warned only after about 52 hours while fraudsters contacted them. Three causes cut across functions: shared accounts without MFA (security), suppliers that cannot support the Club in an incident (compliance and security), and no authority to warn members quickly (governance). See the [integrated case study](https://github.com/HollaAB/ai-governance-portfolio/blob/main/integrated-grc-capstone/03-case-study/integrated-case-study.md).

## 4. Recommendation

The Committee is asked to:

1. Approve the [integrated remediation roadmap](https://github.com/HollaAB/ai-governance-portfolio/blob/main/integrated-grc-capstone/05-findings-and-remediation/remediation-roadmap.md) of 28 open actions in three phases.
2. Amend the delegation of authority so the DPO may send a pre-approved fraud warning to members after a breach, informing the General Manager (IF-03).
3. Require export alerts, an out-of-hours contact and incident support in every critical supplier contract at its next renewal (IF-02).
4. Approve spending of about KES 1,200,000 (scenario estimate) for a spare CCTV recorder, fuel store camera and lighting, and the combined training programme.
5. Decide whether to accept nightly backups from FairwayOne or pay for more frequent backups (IF-05), and record the decision as a risk acceptance if accepted.

## 5. Risks of not acting

The account-takeover risk (R-01) stays outside appetite, and a real phishing attack would likely cause member fraud and ODPC scrutiny.
