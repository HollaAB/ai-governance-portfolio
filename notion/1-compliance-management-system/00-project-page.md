## 1. Executive Summary

Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional members' golf club in Kiambu County, had no single view of what it must comply with. Renewals sat in personal calendars and evidence was spread across departments. I built a compliance management system for the Club: a register of 37 legal, regulatory, contractual and internal obligations (36 apply), each with an owner, a control, a calendar deadline and an evidence item. A gap assessment then tested all 22 controls and found the Club compliant with 18 of 36 obligations, with 16 graded findings and corrective actions. After the follow-up in the later projects, 25 of 36 obligations are compliant and all four High findings are closed (scenario results).

## 2. The Problem

**Problem statement:** The Club could not say which laws and rules applied to it, who was responsible for each one, or how it would prove compliance.

**Why it matters:** With the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner taking public enforcement action, the Management Committee needed assurance that nothing was being missed. A missed licence renewal can close the bars; a missing data protection control can lead to fines and loss of member trust.

## 3. Objectives

The project aims to:

- Identify every obligation that applies to the Club and record why.
- Give each obligation an accountable owner, a control and an evidence item.
- Put every recurring deadline into one compliance calendar.
- Test whether the controls work and record the gaps as graded findings.
- Agree corrective actions with owners and due dates, and report to the Committee.

## 4. Scope and Assumptions

**Included:** Data protection, anti-bribery, employment, health and safety, tax, licensing, environment, corporate and contractual obligations; a gap assessment of all controls; findings, corrective actions, a compliance policy, a monitoring plan and reporting.

**Excluded:** Legal advice, and sector rules that do not apply to a members' golf club.

## 5. Methodology

**My approach:**

1. Listed the Club's activities from the organisation profile and identified the laws, regulations, county laws, contracts and internal rules for each.
2. Assessed applicability and kept non-applicable obligations in the register with the reason, so the decision is visible.
3. Assigned an owner, a control, a calendar entry and an evidence item to every applicable obligation, and checked traceability with validation rules.
4. Tested each control against evidence, sampling where there was a population, and rated each control and obligation.
5. Recorded each exception as a finding with a severity, agreed corrective actions, and reported to the Finance and Audit sub-committee.

**Standards, frameworks, or references used:** ISO 37301:2021 (compliance management systems) as structure; Data Protection Act 2019 and its regulations; Bribery Act 2016; Employment Act 2007; Occupational Safety and Health Act 2007; Fire Risk Reduction Rules 2007; county alcoholic drinks control law; Public Health Act; Pest Control Products Act.

## 7. Testing Results or Evaluation Criteria

All 22 controls were tested against evidence from 12 to 23 October 2026, with samples taken where there was a population (for example 25 personnel files, 20 promotional messages, 30 food handlers' certificates). All results are scenario results.

- **Controls:** 7 tested and effective, 10 tested with exceptions, 5 not implemented.
- **Obligations:** 18 of 36 compliant (50%), 14 partly compliant, 4 not compliant.
- **Findings:** 16 (4 High, 9 Medium, 3 Low), each with a corrective action, owner and due date.
- **Follow-up:** after the Committee's decisions and the data protection programme, 25 of 36 obligations are compliant and 7 findings are closed, including all 4 High.

## 8. Key Findings or Design Decisions

**Finding 1: The gaps sit in new activities, not old duties.** Tax, payroll, corporate filings and most licences were well controlled. The High findings were all in newer areas: no DPIAs for the number-plate recognition pilot and the AI chatbot, processor contracts without data protection terms, and no breach procedure.

**Finding 2: Deadlines in personal calendars fail.** The liquor licence was renewed 9 days late and 6 of 30 food handlers' medical certificates had expired. One shared calendar with reminders to two people fixes both.

**Decision 3: Keep non-applicable obligations in the register.** Recording why an obligation does not apply makes the judgement visible to reviewers and saves the question being asked again.

## 9. Practical Recommendations

| Recommendation | Rationale | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Complete DPIAs, sign processor agreements, approve a breach procedure and hold a fire drill within 30 days | These four High findings carry the largest legal and safety exposure | High |
| Approve the compliance policy and report the seven KPIs to the Finance and Audit sub-committee each quarter | Gives the Committee regular assurance and makes owners accountable | High |
| Move every licence and certificate expiry into the compliance calendar with 60-day reminders | Stops late renewals caused by personal calendars | Medium |
| Start the regulatory change log and assess each change within 10 working days | New legal duties are otherwise picked up informally or missed | Medium |

## 10. Implementation Roadmap

- **Within 30 days (Compliance Officer, Head of Security):** close the four High findings. Done in the scenario by December 2026.
- **By January 2027 (department owners):** finish the retention schedule, gifts register and reporting channel, signed staff contracts, licence calendar, food handlers' certificates and regulatory change log.
- **By April 2027 (department owners):** WhatsApp opt-out line, safety committee dates and workshop risk assessment, pesticide registration checks.
- **Every quarter (Compliance Officer):** retest closed actions, run the 2027 monitoring plan and report the KPIs.

## 11. Skills Demonstrated

- **Regulatory analysis:** turning Kenyan law into specific, owned obligations with an applicability decision for each.
- **Control design:** mapping 36 obligations to 22 controls, a calendar and evidence.
- **Compliance testing:** evidence-based testing with a sampling rule and clear rating scales.
- **Reporting:** graded findings, corrective actions and a management report a board can act on.

## 12. Lessons Learned

A single register showed at once where the risk sits: in newer activities such as AI, number-plate recognition and cloud suppliers, not in long-standing duties like tax. Tying every finding to an obligation and a control made corrective actions easy to assign. In a real organisation, the register would need legal sign-off, a review of leases, insurance and all supplier contracts, and larger samples for some populations.
