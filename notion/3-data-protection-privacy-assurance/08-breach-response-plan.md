## What counts as a breach

Any loss, theft, unauthorised access, disclosure, change or destruction of personal data, whether by attack, mistake or system failure. Examples: a misdirected email with member data, a lost laptop, a hacked account, a supplier incident.

## The 72-hour clock

The clock starts when the Club becomes aware of a breach. A notifiable breach must be reported to the ODPC within 72 hours (Data Protection Act 2019, s.43), and affected people told without undue delay where there is a real risk of harm.

## Steps

| Time | Step | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Immediately | Report to the DPO and the ICT Officer; contain (revoke access, recall email, isolate device) | Whoever finds it |
| Within 4 hours | Open an entry in the [breach log](https://github.com/HollaAB/ai-governance-portfolio/blob/main/data-protection-privacy-assurance/08-breach-response/breach-log.csv); tell the General Manager | DPO |
| Within 24 hours | Assess: what data, how many people, likely harm; decide if notifiable; tell the Chairman if it is | DPO with the ICT Officer |
| Within 72 hours | Notify the ODPC if notifiable, with the facts known so far | DPO |
| As soon as possible | Tell affected people if there is a real risk of harm, with advice on what they can do | DPO and Membership and Events Manager |
| Within 30 days | Root-cause review and corrective actions; report to the Finance and Audit sub-committee | DPO |

## Assessing harm

Notify when the breach is likely to cause real harm: identity fraud, financial loss, distress, discrimination or loss of confidentiality of sensitive data. Record the reasoning for every decision, including decisions not to notify.

## Suppliers

Processors must report breaches to the Club within 24 hours (data processing agreements). The Club, as controller, remains responsible for notifying the ODPC.

## Testing

The plan is tested at least once a year in a tabletop exercise (Stage 5).
