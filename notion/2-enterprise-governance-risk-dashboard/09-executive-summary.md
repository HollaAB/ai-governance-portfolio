> **Portfolio project.** This is an independent learning project using a fictional organisation and illustrative data. It is not an audit, legal opinion, or certification.

All results, dates and decisions are scenario results for the fictional Club.

**The problem.** At Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional members' club, high-risk technology (number-plate recognition and an AI chatbot) went live without Committee review, decisions were minuted but not followed up, and nobody had agreed how much risk the Club would accept.

**What was done.** The Club's governance was scored against the eleven ISO 37000:2021 principles (baseline 2.5 out of 5). A governance framework, delegation of authority, refreshed terms of reference, RACI and escalation matrices, a committee skills matrix and a risk appetite statement were set up. The Stage 2 compliance results were then taken through a full oversight cycle: board paper, Committee decisions, actions with owners and deadlines, and closure evidence. Eighteen risks were compared with the appetite, and nine key risk indicators and a quarterly dashboard were set up.

**What was found.** 9 of 18 risks were outside appetite. Eight governance findings were raised; four are closed through the Committee's October decisions. After one quarter, maturity rose to 3.0, and two of the four High compliance findings are closed.

**What is recommended.** Collect conflict of interest declarations in January 2027, obtain the legal opinion on caddies, co-opt a member with technology skills, and keep the dashboard as a standing item at every quarterly meeting.

**What this demonstrates.** Designing governance that produces decisions and follow-through, not only policies; linking risk appetite to escalation; and reporting compliance, risk and governance on one page a board can use.
