## 1. Executive Summary

Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional Kenyan members' club, processed member, staff and visitor data across cloud systems, CCTV, number-plate recognition and an AI chatbot without a record of processing, impact assessments, processor agreements, a retention schedule or a breach plan. I built a data protection programme under the Data Protection Act 2019: 14 processing activities recorded and mapped, two DPIAs approved with conditions, processor agreements and a transfer assessment, a retention schedule, a privacy notice, a request procedure and a breach response plan. A follow-up review found five of six earlier data protection findings closed, CCTV retention cut from 180 to 30 days, and every request in the quarter answered on time (scenario results). Five smaller findings remain, mainly two missing processor agreements and staff training.

## 2. The Problem

**Problem statement:** The compliance review found the Club's biggest gaps in data protection: no record of processing, no DPIAs for number-plate recognition or the chatbot, processor contracts without data protection terms, no retention schedule and no breach procedure.

**Why it matters:** The Club holds data on about 1,850 members and their dependants, 165 staff, caddies and visitors, some of it hosted in South Africa or processed by an AI vendor. Without these controls it could not show that its processing was lawful, and a breach could go unreported past the 72-hour deadline.

## 3. Objectives

The project aims to:

- Record every processing activity with its purpose, lawful basis, systems, recipients and retention.
- Assess the high-risk processing before it continues.
- Put every processor and cross-border transfer under a written agreement and safeguards.
- Set and apply retention periods.
- Make members' rights and breach response work in practice.

## 4. Scope and Assumptions

**Included:** Personal data inventory and data-flow map; DPIAs for gate CCTV with number-plate recognition and for the Kijani Assist chatbot; DPIA procedure; processor register and transfer assessment; retention schedule and disposal log; privacy notice and CCTV signs; data subject request procedure and log; breach response plan and log; a privacy assurance review.

**Excluded:** AI-specific governance of the chatbot, which is covered in the AI Governance Framework project; legal advice.

## 5. Methodology

**My approach:**

1. Interviewed each department and recorded every processing activity in a personal data inventory, which serves as the record of processing activities.
2. Mapped how data moves between the Club, its systems and its processors.
3. Screened for high-risk processing and completed a DPIA for each high-risk activity, with risks scored before and after measures.
4. Checked every processor for a written agreement and assessed each transfer outside Kenya.
5. Set retention periods, published notices, set up the request and breach processes, and tested them in a privacy assurance review.

**Standards, frameworks, or references used:** Data Protection Act 2019 and the Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021; Office of the Data Protection Commissioner guidance on DPIAs (structure followed).

## 7. Testing Results or Evaluation Criteria

A privacy assurance review tested the new controls. All results are scenario results.

- **Earlier findings:** 5 of 6 data protection findings from the compliance review closed; retention still in progress.
- **Record of processing:** 14 of 14 activities recorded with a lawful basis.
- **DPIAs:** 3 of 3 high-risk activities covered by an approved DPIA.
- **Processors:** 4 of 6 under a written agreement.
- **Requests:** 4 of 4 answered on time.
- **Breaches:** one minor breach assessed within 24 hours and correctly judged not notifiable.
- **New findings:** 5 (3 Medium, 2 Low).

## 8. Key Findings or Design Decisions

**Finding 1: Most risk came from new technology adopted without assessment.** Number-plate recognition and the chatbot went live with no DPIA. Both were assessed and approved with conditions.

**Decision 2: Reduce risk without stopping the service.** Simple measures cut residual risk sharply: number-plate recognition became opt-in at the main gate only, CCTV retention fell from 180 to 30 days, and no account data is sent to the model provider.

**Finding 3: Supplier oversight is still incomplete.** The guarding company and the events partner handle personal data without a written agreement. These two gaps are the main open items.

## 9. Practical Recommendations

| Recommendation | Rationale | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Sign a data processing agreement with Linda Guard Services | Guards view CCTV and keep the visitor register without an agreement | High |
| Finish disposal of former member records from 2019 to 2021 | About 300 records are past their retention period | Medium |
| Train all staff who handle personal data and add it to induction | Reception, gate and events staff have had no training in two years | Medium |
| Sign an agreement with the events partner, or share only names and numbers | Guest lists, sometimes with dietary needs, are shared without an agreement | Low |

## 10. Implementation Roadmap

- **By January 2027 (Head of Security, Compliance Officer):** agreement with Linda Guard Services; finish disposal of old records.
- **By March 2027 (Membership and Events Manager, HR Manager):** events partner agreement; staff data protection training.
- **Every month (Compliance Officer):** review the request log, breach log and CCTV viewing log.
- **Before any new high-risk processing (Compliance Officer):** run the DPIA procedure.

## 11. Skills Demonstrated

- **Data protection compliance:** applying the Kenyan Data Protection Act to a real-world operation, including lawful bases and retention.
- **Privacy impact assessment:** two DPIAs with residual risk measured before and after measures.
- **Third-party risk:** processor agreements and a cross-border transfer assessment.
- **Assurance:** a follow-up review that tests whether the controls work.

## 12. Lessons Learned

Most data protection risk came from new technology adopted without assessment, not from long-standing processing. Simple, practical measures cut residual risk without stopping the services members use. A real club would take legal advice on consent for number-plate recognition and on the balancing test for CCTV.

## 13. Next Steps

- Close the four open actions by March 2027.
- Review both DPIAs whenever the services change, and the chatbot DPIA at the Committee's June 2027 review of Kijani Assist.
- Feed supplier and training gaps into the integrated capstone roadmap.
