# Methodology: Data Protection and Privacy Assurance

Prepared by Abel Holla.

1. **Inventory.** Interview each department and list every processing activity: data subjects, data categories, purpose, lawful basis, source, systems, recipients, transfers and retention. Record it in the [personal data inventory](../03-data-inventory/personal-data-inventory.csv), which serves as the record of processing activities.
2. **Map flows.** Draw how data moves between the Club, its systems and its processors in the [data-flow map](../03-data-inventory/data-flow-map.md).
3. **Screen for DPIAs.** Mark activities that are high-risk: systematic monitoring, sensitive data, automated decisions, new technology, or large volumes. Complete a DPIA for each before it runs (or, for the two that were already running, before they continue).
4. **Check processors and transfers.** List every processor, confirm a written agreement, and assess each transfer outside Kenya.
5. **Set retention.** Give every record type a retention period, a trigger and a disposal method.
6. **Inform and respond.** Publish a privacy notice and CCTV signs; set up the request procedure and log.
7. **Prepare for breaches.** Write the breach response plan, open the breach log, and test the assessment step on a real minor incident.
8. **Review.** Test the new controls in a privacy assurance review, record findings and corrective actions, and report.

## Lawful bases used

The Data Protection Act 2019 allows processing on the bases listed in s.30, including consent, performance of a contract, a legal obligation, vital interests, the public interest and legitimate interests. Each activity in the inventory names one main basis.

## Reference

Data Protection Act 2019 and its 2021 regulations; Office of the Data Protection Commissioner guidance on DPIAs (structure followed). Legal references were checked against the official texts by Abel Holla (October 2026).
