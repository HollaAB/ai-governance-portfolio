## 1. Executive Summary

At Kijani Heights Golf and Country Club, a fictional members' golf club, number-plate recognition and an AI chatbot went live without Committee review, decisions were minuted but not followed up, and nobody had agreed how much risk the Club would accept. I scored the Club's governance against the eleven ISO 37000:2021 principles (baseline 2.5 out of 5), then designed a governance framework, delegation of authority, accountability matrices and a risk appetite statement. The compliance results from the previous project were taken through a full oversight cycle: board paper, Committee decisions, actions with owners and deadlines, and closure evidence. After one quarter, maturity rose to 3.0, four of eight governance findings were closed, and two of the four High compliance findings were closed through Committee decisions (scenario results).

## 2. The Problem

**Problem statement:** The Club had no rule on who approves what, no agreed risk appetite, and no way of tracking Committee decisions to completion.

**Why it matters:** The compliance review traced several of its biggest gaps to these governance failures. High-risk technology was approved by management alone, so nobody asked for a data protection impact assessment. Without follow-up, Committee decisions did not change anything.

## 3. Objectives

The project aims to:

- Set out who decides what, with financial and risk limits.
- Agree a risk appetite and compare every enterprise risk with it.
- Track every Committee decision to closure evidence.
- Report compliance, risk and governance on one quarterly dashboard.

## 4. Scope and Assumptions

**Included:** Governance framework, delegation of authority, sub-committee terms of reference, conflict of interest declarations, RACI, escalation and skills matrices, risk appetite, enterprise risk register and key risk indicators, a board oversight trail, governance findings, and a quarterly dashboard and report.

**Excluded:** Changes to the Club's Articles, and legal advice (including on the caddie engagement question, which is recorded only as a risk and a decision).

## 5. Methodology

**My approach:**

1. Reviewed the Articles, Bylaws, a year of Committee minutes and the compliance findings, and scored current practice against the eleven ISO 37000 principles on a 1 to 5 scale.
2. Designed the governance structure, reserved matters and delegation of authority, and refreshed the terms of reference of the Finance and Audit and the Security and Safety sub-committees.
3. Mapped 13 key processes to 9 roles in a RACI matrix and set an escalation matrix that says what goes to whom and how fast.
4. Wrote a risk appetite statement by risk category, compared all 18 enterprise risks with it, and defined 9 key risk indicators with green, amber and red thresholds.
5. Took the compliance results through a full oversight cycle and reported the results on a quarterly dashboard.

**Standards, frameworks, or references used:** ISO 37000:2021 (governance of organisations) for the principles and maturity assessment; ISO 31000:2018 (risk management) for risk appetite and escalation.

## 7. Testing Results or Evaluation Criteria

Governance was evaluated against the eleven ISO 37000 principles before and after one quarter. All results are scenario results.

- **Maturity:** baseline 2.5 out of 5, with risk governance lowest (1); 3.0 after one quarter.
- **Risk:** 9 of 18 risks outside appetite at baseline. Later projects re-scored several risks; 6 remain outside appetite.
- **Oversight:** 8 Committee decisions recorded on 28 October 2026, each with an owner, deadline and closure evidence; 71% of actions closed on time in the quarter.
- **Findings:** 8 governance findings (2 High, 4 Medium, 2 Low); 4 closed by the Committee's decisions.
- **Effect on compliance:** 2 of the 4 High compliance findings closed through Committee follow-up.

## 8. Key Findings or Design Decisions

**Finding 1: Compliance gaps had a governance cause.** Without a delegation of authority, the General Manager approved number-plate recognition and the chatbot alone, so nobody asked for an impact assessment. Adopting a delegation of authority closed that route.

**Finding 2: Decisions without follow-up change nothing.** Decisions were minuted but never tracked. A decision register and action register, reviewed at every meeting, made progress visible.

**Decision 3: Link risk appetite to escalation.** Each risk category has an appetite level, and the escalation matrix says who must be told when a risk or indicator moves outside it. This turns the appetite statement into a working rule.

## 9. Practical Recommendations

| Recommendation | Rationale | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the dashboard as a standing item at every quarterly Committee meeting | Keeps compliance, risk and governance in one view the Committee acts on | High |
| Collect conflict of interest declarations from January 2027 | 11 declarations are outstanding and two Committee members are supplier directors | High |
| Obtain a legal opinion on the caddie engagement model | Risk R-09 had no owner or control; a misclassification claim could be costly | Medium |
| Co-opt a Committee member with technology or data protection skills | The Committee scores below 2.5 in technology and compliance | Medium |
| Consult members before any high-risk service launch | Members were not told about number-plate recognition or the chatbot | Low |

## 10. Implementation Roadmap

- **28 October 2026 (Management Committee):** risk appetite, delegation of authority and terms of reference adopted. Done in the scenario.
- **By January 2027 (General Manager):** collect conflict of interest declarations; obtain the caddie legal opinion.
- **By March 2027 (Chairman, Membership and Events Manager):** co-opt a member with technology skills; start member consultation and DPIA summaries.
- **Every quarter (General Manager):** review the decision and action registers and the dashboard.

## 11. Skills Demonstrated

- **Governance design:** a framework, delegation of authority and terms of reference that produce decisions.
- **Risk governance:** risk appetite by category, escalation rules and key risk indicators.
- **Board reporting:** a board paper, minutes and a one-page dashboard.
- **Assessment:** a maturity assessment against ISO 37000 principles with findings and actions.

## 12. Lessons Learned

Governance becomes visible only when decisions are tracked through to closure evidence. The compliance gaps from the previous project had a governance cause, so fixing the approval rule prevented the same problem returning. In a real club, financial limits and indicator thresholds would be set from its own budget and history, and the caddie question would need a real legal opinion.

## 13. Next Steps

- Close the four open governance actions by March 2027.
- Report the dashboard each quarter and re-score maturity after a year.
- Bring the decisions proposed in the capstone to the Committee in February 2027.
